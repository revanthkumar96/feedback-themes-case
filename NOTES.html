Customer feedback themes

An evidence-backed pipeline that discovers a subject taxonomy, freezes it, and assigns reviews to stable leaf IDs. The model handles semantic judgement; ordinary code owns identity, hierarchy, evidence validation, retry limits, accounting, and output projection.

Theme set and how I arrived at it

I selected two disjoint samples of 40 reviews. Each sample contains eight reviews from every rating and deliberately spans short and long feedback. This controls the dataset's strong one-star/five-star imbalance without allowing rating to become a theme. GPT-OSS 20B at low reasoning produced two independent candidate trees.

The candidate trees were useful but not acceptable as final taxonomies. One overfit incidents and support channels; the other created mirror labels such as “fast approval” and “slow approval,” encoding polarity in the taxonomy. A GPT-OSS 120B consolidation removed some duplication but collapsed distinct subjects into vague “overview” leaves and introduced many one-child hierarchy layers. I therefore treated all three outputs as research material and performed the final taxonomy review myself.

The frozen set contains 31 polarity-neutral specific subjects under 14 midlevel and four strategic themes. Important boundary decisions include separating fee level from fee transparency, portal access from usability and performance, response speed from follow-up ownership, and interest level from calculation. A positive and negative review use the same subject label.

The final distribution supports the chosen granularity: the median specific theme has eight rows, only one of 31 leaves is a singleton, and the largest strategic theme contains 35% rather than dominating the tree.

Decisions the brief left open

  • Theme identity: stable snake-case IDs are canonical; labels and definitions can evolve without breaking assignments.
  • Multi-label behaviour: every independently supported subject is returned, up to a safety ceiling of five. The ceiling is stated in the prompt itself: a seven-subject review demonstrated that enforcing a limit the model has never been told about turns honest output into a validation failure.
  • Abstention: an empty assignment list means the review contains no supported subject. The rich output derives an explicit no_relevant_theme reason.
  • Evidence: every assignment carries one contiguous source span. Unknown IDs, duplicates, paraphrased evidence, missing reviews, or reordered results fail the batch.
  • Hierarchy: the model emits only a specific-theme ID. Midlevel and strategic parents are looked up from the frozen registry, making model-created tree contradictions impossible.
  • Provider configuration: 20-review responses were not reliably schema-complete. Ten-review batches cleared that failure mode and remained comfortably inside the time gate.
  • Semantic evaluation: the supplied checker verifies structure, not faithfulness. A 50-review rating-stratified holdout, disjoint from the discovery samples wherever the strata allow, was annotated against the frozen definitions and drives precision/recall, abstention, and stability measurement (python -m feedback_themes holdout / evaluate).

Rich result + flat projection

The primary out/results.json is normalized: it contains run metadata, the exact taxonomy, and review assignments referencing leaf IDs with evidence. out/flat.json is a deterministic, intentionally lossy join used by the supplied checker.

Calls are sequential because the free-tier token-per-minute limit, not model inference, controls latency. HTTP 429 responses are retried after the server-provided delay, including millisecond and minute-granularity guidance. Schema-generation and semantic retries are separately bounded and counted. Every successful batch is atomically checkpointed; a checkpoint is reused only when review IDs, taxonomy hash, prompt version, model, reasoning setting, completion limit, and batch size all match, and a stale checkpoint is reported and recomputed rather than silently mixed in.

An embedding-assisted retrieval mode (BGE-small via fastembed) exists as an opt-in experiment behind --hybrid and an optional dependency extra. A preliminary measurement showed candidate lists at top-12 retained only 79.2% of the reference assignments, so it is not part of the submitted configuration; the submitted run gives the model the full 31-leaf taxonomy.

What I tried that did not work

  • Medium reasoning was useful for fixed classification but repeatedly consumed the taxonomy-generation budget before returning JSON. Discovery moved to 20B-low.
  • Strict JSON mode did not prevent every provider-side schema failure. Removing a redundant nullable reason field simplified the model contract, while local semantic validation remained authoritative.
  • A 20-review batch omitted a required field. Ten-review batches were stable and still completed well below 25 minutes.
  • The first full 20B run exhausted a development daily-token allowance after 110 reviews. That exposed the need for batch checkpoints. The 120B-low challenger then recovered more known secondary themes and earned selection for the final run.
  • Model-generated taxonomy consolidation was not trusted blindly. The final human review removed polarity pairs, vague “overview” nodes, and unsupported hierarchy layers.
  • The validator capped assignments at five, but the prompt never said so. A review that legitimately discusses seven subjects made the model enumerate them all — correct behaviour that failed validation three times. The ceiling is now disclosed in the prompt with an instruction to keep the five most explicit subjects.
  • Two rate-limit retries with second-granularity parsing were not enough. A burst of three 429s killed a full run, and “try again in 240ms” guidance parsed to nothing. The client now honours ms/s/min guidance with six bounded retries, and a daily-quota exhaustion mid-run was absorbed by checkpoint resume rather than a restart.

Measured semantic quality

The structural checker passes equally for faithful and unfaithful output, so quality claims below come from the annotated holdout: 50 rating-stratified reviews scored against frozen theme definitions. The v5 prompt encodes boundary rules derived from the baseline's measured failure patterns — unattributed price complaints, sentiment-only reviews, dropped secondary subjects, and the interest-family boundaries — as generic rules, not review-specific hints.

The boundary rules cut false positives without costing recall; the cheaper 20B model matched precision but lost a third of true assignments and recovered only 42.9% of multi-subject reviews against 67.9% for 120B, so the larger model kept the final run. A repeated 120B-v5 run produced identical assignment sets on 80% of holdout reviews with a mean Jaccard overlap of 0.877 and an unchanged micro-F1 (0.736 vs 0.734) — evidence that the measured quality is not a sampling fluke. Evidence validity is 1.000 in every configuration because verbatim spans are enforced by code, not the model. The submitted v5.1 run — v5 plus the disclosed five-assignment ceiling — lifted multi-subject recall from 0.679 to 0.821 and recall to 0.754, and two failures documented against the baseline (an effective-rate discrepancy misread as a rate change, and a chatbot dead-end misread as portal usability) were verified fixed in the final output.

What I would do with another week

The holdout confirmed that the dominant remaining errors are taxonomy coverage, not classification: account administration, user and role management, and credit enquiries recur in reviews the pipeline can only abstain on. The first revision would add those leaves and re-annotate. I would then grow the holdout beyond 50 reviews, add a second independent annotator to measure agreement, and only adopt further prompt rules when a failure pattern repeats across annotators.

The embedding pre-filter stays parked: it is implemented and measurable, but a 79.2% candidate-recall ceiling means it would cap exactly the recall the 120B model wins on, while the demonstrated errors are coverage and boundary problems no retriever solves. With 223 short reviews and cents of API cost, the added dependency buys nothing yet.
